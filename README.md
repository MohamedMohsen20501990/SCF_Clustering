# Customer Segmentation in the US

## 📌 Overview

This project applies **unsupervised machine learning** techniques to segment U.S. households into distinct customer groups using data from the **2019 Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF)**. The primary goal is to identify patterns in household financial characteristics that can support customer profiling, targeted marketing, and socioeconomic analysis.

The project demonstrates a complete clustering workflow, including data preprocessing, feature selection, dimensionality reduction, and model development using **K-Means Clustering**.

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

* Explore household financial characteristics through exploratory data analysis (EDA).
* Compare customer groups using side-by-side visualizations.
* Select informative features based on variance.
* Reduce dimensionality using **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**.
* Build and evaluate a **K-Means clustering** model.
* Identify meaningful customer segments for business and research applications.

---

## 📊 Dataset

The project uses data from the **2019 Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF)** conducted by the **Federal Reserve Board**.

The SCF is one of the most comprehensive surveys of household finances in the United States, collecting information on:

* Household assets and liabilities
* Income and wealth
* Retirement accounts and pensions
* Debt and credit
* Demographic characteristics
* Financial behavior

The survey provides a nationally representative snapshot of the financial condition of U.S. families and is widely used by economists, researchers, policymakers, and financial institutions.

## 📚 About the Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF)

The **Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF)** is a triennial survey sponsored by the **Federal Reserve Board** in cooperation with the **U.S. Department of the Treasury** and conducted by the **National Opinion Research Center (NORC) at the University of Chicago**.

The survey is designed to provide detailed information on the financial characteristics of U.S. households. A carefully designed sampling process ensures representation across all income and wealth levels, making the SCF one of the most trusted sources for household financial data.

Participation in the survey is voluntary, and strict confidentiality standards are maintained to protect respondents' privacy.
